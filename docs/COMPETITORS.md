# Digital SAT Prep — Competitive Landscape

**Researched June 2026.** Prices verified on vendor sites where possible; review-site-only figures are marked (~). Companion to [COMMERCIALIZATION.md](COMMERCIALIZATION.md).

## TL;DR

The space is crowded — roughly **30 active competitors across five layers**, anchored by a genuinely strong free tier. The free layer (Khan Academy + College Board's own Bluebook and Question Bank) covers official content almost completely but has a hard ceiling: exactly 8 official practice tests and a finite question pool that motivated students exhaust in 4–8 weeks, with shallow explanations and no real error-tracking. Every paid product lives in that gap. The paid B2C layer is the most contested (a dozen AI-native apps at $8–$149/mo, plus legacy brands at $129–$7,500), which is precisely why our recommended wedge remains **B2B white-label to tutors** — where there are only ~3 serious players — with consumer self-serve as the second motion, not the first.

## Layer 0 — Free & official (the baseline everyone competes against)

| Product | Price | What's free | The ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Khan Academy** (official College Board partner) | $0 forever | Full course: thousands of leveled questions, videos, practice plans, Bluebook score walkthroughs | Rules-based personalization, not adaptive AI; no error-log mechanic; generic explanations |
| **College Board Bluebook** | $0 | 8 full-length adaptive official tests (Tests 4–11) — the test-day gold standard | Only 8 tests; 1.4★ on iOS; practice tool, not a learning system |
| **CB SAT Suite Question Bank** | $0 | ~3,500 official questions, filterable, with rationales | Raw pool; no sequencing, tracking, or pedagogy |
| **Schoolhouse.world** | $0 (nonprofit, official partner) | Live 4-week peer-tutored bootcamps (118K+ learners) | Group schedule, peer (not expert) tutors |
| **OnePrep** | $0 core (freemium creep post-acquisition) | 3–4K official questions in a Bluebook-clone UI | Rehosts CB content (legal gray area); reported quality decline |
| **Google Gemini SAT practice** (w/ Princeton Review, launched Jan 2026) | $0 | On-demand full-length practice + AI feedback inside Gemini | New; big-tech commoditizer to watch — validates AI-prep demand, threatens paid diagnostics |

## What this means for us

1. **The manager's instinct is correct** — paid B2C is saturated, and the free baseline is strong and getting stronger (Gemini's free Princeton-Review-vetted practice launched January 2026). A new paid consumer app fighting Khan + a dozen AI startups on question volume would lose.
2. **The free tier's gap is structural, not accidental:** 8 official tests, finite official bank, no teach-on-miss mechanic, no error-log graduation. Students who exhaust the official pool in 4–8 weeks are exactly the high-intent segment that pays.
3. **Nobody owns our mechanic.** Competitors sell volume (8–28K questions) and AI chat tutors. None build the product around *re-proving every miss* (2-in-a-row graduation) with per-error trap teaching. It's a defensible pedagogical position — "the app for your last 100 points" — not a question-volume war we'd lose.
4. **The B2B/tutor layer has ~3 serious players** vs ~15 in B2C. EdisonOS charges $299–1,499/yr for *infrastructure*; a white-label with actual pedagogy (our loop + AI generation at ~⅓¢/question) slots between EdisonOS and Test Innovators. This remains the recommended wedge.
5. **Pricing sanity check:** our modeled $39–49 test-date pass sits under every AI-native monthly plan and above impulse-app pricing — consistent with where the market clusters.

## The College Board copyright question

**Short answer: we don't use College Board questions — and nobody can buy the right to.**

1. **Our bank contains zero College Board content.** All 52 questions were written from scratch (and AI-generated additions are produced by our own pipeline, not from CB material). The independent content audit verified every item; provenance is documented. This was a deliberate day-one decision, not an oversight.
2. **There is no paid licensing route — at any price.** College Board's own permission instructions state: *"SAT test materials are NOT available for commercial license"* and reproduction of official materials *"is not allowed in test prep"* — and they explicitly define test-prep settings as commercial use. Their permission process covers narrow noncommercial educational uses only.
3. **They specifically prohibit AI use of their content:** the permission form states College Board does not grant permission for its content, *including practice questions*, to be used "in conjunction with generative AI" or to train any AI system. Our AI question generator never touches CB content — which this clause makes mandatory, not just prudent.
4. **The only sanctioned channel is Khan Academy** — a free, mission-based nonprofit partnership, not a license a commercial company can replicate. No commercial prep company licenses real SAT items today; companies like Ivy Global license their *own* proprietary tests, not official ones.
5. **Enforcement is real:** College Board sued Karen Dillard's College Prep (2008) over pirated test questions; KDCP settled for **$1 million**. CB also cancelled scores in the 2025 digital-leak incidents.
6. **Original content is the industry standard.** UWorld, Princeton Review, Kaplan, Magoosh, Acely — every commercial player writes "exam-style" original questions for exactly this reason. We follow the same model.

So the copyright posture is: **no fees owed, no license needed, no CB content present** — the cost shows up instead as content authoring + expert review (~$2–5K for a 1,000-item bank), already in the commercialization plan. The trademark side (the word "SAT" in a product name) is a separate issue, already covered: rename before commercial launch + the standard disclaimer.
